Clear the cursors and return to the full trace. Step 6 checks concurrency: migrations, locks, and priority inheritance.

The trace has about 19,000 migrations. Task CS[18] migrates about 1,560 times per second, its average dwell on a core is about half a millisecond, and its ping-pong count is high. This is migration thrashing. It does not by itself prove lock contention; common causes are task affinity or many similar worker tasks. A lock bounce is different: it is when a task moves to another core while it still holds a lock.

In Task View, lock the highlight on CS[18]. With Load enabled, the chart shows its CPU share on each core. Then open Mutex and Queue. One mutex has 864 holds and 9 cross-core bounces, so its status is Warning. One queue has a stronger signal: 858 of its 864 holds moved across cores while still held, though its status may still read OK. Check each warning on the timeline before you call it a defect or just cleanup activity.
