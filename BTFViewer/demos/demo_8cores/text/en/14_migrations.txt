The trace has about 19,000 migrations. Task CS[20] migrates about 1,640 times per second, its average dwell on a core is about half a millisecond, and its ping-pong count is high. This is migration thrashing. It does not by itself prove lock contention; common causes are task affinity or many similar worker tasks. A lock bounce is different: it is when a task moves to another core while it still holds a lock.

In Task View, lock the highlight on CS[20]. With Load enabled, the chart shows its CPU share on each core. Then open Mutex and Queue. One queue has 864 holds and 848 cross-core bounces: nearly every hold moved to another core while the object was still held, so its status is Warning. The mutexes in this trace are used lightly and read OK. Check each warning on the timeline before you call it a defect or just cleanup activity.
