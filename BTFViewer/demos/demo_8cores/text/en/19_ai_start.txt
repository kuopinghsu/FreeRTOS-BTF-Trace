Read the Assistant reply in three blocks. First, the finding: Confirmed priority inversion on P-S two twenty-eight. Direct evidence is a priority-inheritance boost in that cursor window: base priority two raised to four because of an L-M-H pattern with C-S eleven and C-S twelve. Second, the causal chain: the task was on C-P-U, hit a mutex, got the boost at jump three million fifty-five thousand thirty-three — about three point zero five five seconds on this file — then migrated from core seven to core six at jump three million fifty-five thousand two hundred twenty-six. Third, dependencies: P-S two twenty-eight inherits from C-S eleven through fourteen, so this is shared-lock contention, not a one-off glitch. The stated root cause is higher-priority C-S tasks holding the mutex and delaying P-S two twenty-eight; bouncing cores while the lock is held can make it worse.

The Evidence panel is the host, not the model. It should name Current Issue as priority inversion on P-S two twenty-eight, list why each tool ran, and show cost — here about one hundred twenty-nine thousand tokens and eleven tools. It also lists what would disprove the story: no mutex hold in the window, the delay while the task is already runnable, or another waiter causing most of the block. Click every jump link and check Priority Inheritance and the red boost stripe. The model may say High confidence; still open Mutex Blocking and Critical Path, and click p-ninety-five if you want the distribution. Treat the what-if as an estimate only.

That is the newbie path: start from a named finding, watch evidence land on the timeline, then confirm or reject it in Statistics. The next run can pick a different top finding and tell a different story.
